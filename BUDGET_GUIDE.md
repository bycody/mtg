# Budget Standard Guide

## Key conclusions

- Prefer an archetype that is inexpensive *by design* over removing essential engine cards from a $400 deck.
- Blue tempo/flash and Izzet spells can be inexpensive; fully optimized control lists are often harder to budget.
- Mono-color saves on lands. Two-color decks should prioritize reliable untapped mana before optional sideboard luxuries.
- Identify **core engine**, **interaction**, **mana**, and **flex slots**. Cut flex slots first.
- Price the complete 60 + 15, then compare results at local events rather than assuming budget means competitive.
- Check legality and bans on purchase day; 2025–2026 deck guides may have banned cards.
- Buy cheapest tournament-legal printings; avoid collectible alternate art premiums.

## Specific deck recommendations

1. **Mono-Blue Flash** — best starting point for blue, budget, and reactive play. ~$76 historical listing; no demonstrated top-tier performance.
2. **Izzet Spellementals** — tournament-proven archetype; exact Nov 1, 2026 list ~$146. Test budget mana substitutions, but don't assume equal performance.
3. **Zero-Rare Izzet Lessons** — historical study only. Four copies of Gran-Gran are banned; removing them changes the engine.

## Before buying

1. Verify the exact 75 cards against current Standard legality.
2. Refresh per-card prices using Scryfall/TCGplayer/MTGGoldfish; include shipping and tax.
3. Proxy or test 10–20 matches first, recording win rate, mana issues, and dead draws.
4. Upgrade only cards tied to a measured problem.

## Pricing data quality

Deck-level estimates are sourced snapshots. Only Izzet Spellementals has verified individual per-card prices from the accessible source. The other files explicitly leave individual prices unverified rather than inventing them.
